Special Report: Fact-Checking Policy Claims

Analyzing the State of the Nation Address

Published July 29, 2026 • Written by Babajide Okonjo

In the wake of the latest ministerial briefings in Abuja, public discussions have centered on foreign reserves, energy costs, and infrastructure development. Policy critics and administration advocates have released contrasting numbers, highlighting the need for real-time verification of statistical claims in the public square.

"Our administration has shown fiscal discipline that is yielding results. For example, Nigeria's external reserves increased by $10 billion in the last six months alone."
— Ministerial Press Briefing

Another major talking point was the fiscal impact of fuel pricing policies. A prominent government representative stated: The government has fully eliminated the fuel subsidy, saving 2 trillion Naira. They argued that these savings are already being redistributed to state governors to build rural roads and healthcare clinics.

On inflation and the cost of living, opposition leaders were quick to highlight economic pressures. In a counter-press conference, they asserted that Nigeria's inflation rate dropped to 12% in the second quarter. They blamed monetary policies and currency adjustments for the current market prices.

Finally, regarding capital projects, supporters of the Ministry of Works pointed to the coastal transport initiative. In a viral social media post, a coalition stated that The 700-kilometer Lagos-Calabar coastal highway has been completely paved and commissioned. This claim was widely shared to demonstrate infrastructural achievements.
